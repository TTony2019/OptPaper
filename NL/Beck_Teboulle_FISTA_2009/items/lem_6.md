## Lean statement
```lean
theorem fista_t_lower_bound (k : ℕ) (hk : 1 ≤ k) :
    fistaTSeq k ≥ ((k : ℝ) + 1) / 2
```

## Statement explanation
The declaration is a theorem named `fista_t_lower_bound`. It takes a natural number \(k\) and a hypothesis `hk` asserting that \(1 \le k\). The sequence `fistaTSeq` is a real-valued sequence \(t:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R\), defined by
\[
t_0=1,\qquad t_1=1,\qquad
t_{k+2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{k+1}^2}}{2}\quad(k\in\mathbb N).
\]
Equivalently, for every \(j\ge 1\),
\[
t_{j+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_j^2}}{2}.
\]
The conclusion of the theorem is
\[
t_k \ge \frac{k+1}{2},
\]
where the natural number \(k\) is coerced to a real number on the right-hand side. Thus the theorem says that for every integer \(k\ge 1\), the \(k\)-th term of this FISTA-type sequence is at least \((k+1)/2\). The accompanying docstring identifies this as Lemma 4.3: the positive sequence generated by the FISTA recurrence with \(t_1=1\) satisfies \(t_k \ge (k+1)/2\) for all \(k\ge 1\). No further assumptions are made.

    have hm_pos : m ≠ 0 := by omega

    have hi' : fistaTSeq (n + 1) ≥ (((n : ℝ) + 1) + 1) / 2 := by
      simpa [Nat.cast_succ] using hi

    have hn_nonneg : 0 ≤ (n : ℝ) := Nat.cast_nonneg _
    have hn2_nonneg : 0 ≤ (n : ℝ) + 2 := by linarith
    have hS_nonneg : 0 ≤ fistaTSeq (n + 1) := by
      have : 0 ≤ ((n : ℝ) + 2) / 2 := by nlinarith

## Proof explanation
The proof proceeds by lower induction on the natural number \(k\). The tactic `refine Nat.le_induction ?base ?step k hk` applies the principle that, in order to prove a statement \(P(k)\) for all \(k\) with \(1\le k\), it suffices to prove \(P(1)\) and to prove that \(P(m)\) implies \(P(m+1)\) whenever \(1\le m\). Here the statement is
\[
P(k):\quad t_k \ge \frac{k+1}{2}.
\]

For the base case \(k=1\), the command `simp [fistaTSeq]` unfolds the definition of the sequence and evaluates \(t_1=1\). The goal becomes
\[
1 \ge \frac{1+1}{2}=1,
\]
which is true by simplification.

For the induction step, the proof introduces a natural number \(m\), the hypothesis \(1\le m\), and the induction hypothesis
\[
hi:\quad t_m \ge \frac{m+1}{2}.
\]
It must prove
\[
P(m+1):\quad t_{m+1} \ge \frac{m+2}{2}.
\]
The command `omega` uses the hypothesis \(1\le m\) to derive \(m\ne 0\). Then `Nat.exists_eq_succ_of_ne_zero hm_pos` gives a natural number \(n\) with \(m=n+1\), and the `rfl` pattern substitutes \(m\) by \(n+1\). Thus the induction hypothesis becomes
\[
t_{n+1} \ge \frac{(n+1)+1}{2}=\frac{n+2}{2}.
\]
The tactic `simpa [Nat.cast_succ] using hi` rewrites the coercion \(((n+1):\mathbb R)\) as \((n:\mathbb R)+1\), producing the stored inequality `hi'`. The goal is now
\[
t_{n+2} \ge \frac{(n+2)+1}{2}=\frac{n+3}{2}.
\]
The command `simp [fistaTSeq, Nat.cast_succ]` unfolds the recurrence for \(t_{n+2}\), replacing it by
\[
t_{n+2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{n+1}^2}}{2},
\]
and simplifies the casts in the right-hand side. So the goal becomes
\[
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{n+1}^2}}{2}\ge \frac{n+3}{2}.
\]

Next the proof establishes the needed nonnegativity facts. `Nat.cast_nonneg` gives \(0\le (n:\mathbb R)\). From this, `linarith` proves \(0\le (n:\mathbb R)+2\). Since
\[
t_{n+1}\ge \frac{n+2}{2}
\]
and \((n+2)/2\ge 0\), the proof obtains
\[
0\le t_{n+1}.
\]
This is the purpose of `hS_nonneg`: the lower bound on \(t_{n+1}\) and the nonnegativity of \((n+2)/2\) imply that \(t_{n+1}\) itself is nonnegative.

The proof then squares the lower bound. From
\[
t_{n+1}\ge \frac{n+2}{2}
\]
and the nonnegativity of both sides, `nlinarith` proves
\[
t_{n+1}^2 \ge \left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^2.
\]
Multiplying by \(4\) gives
\[
4t_{n+1}^2 \ge 4\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^2.
\]
The algebraic identity
\[
4\left(\frac{n+2}{2}\right)^2=(n+2)^2
\]
is verified by `ring`, so the proof obtains
\[
4t_{n+1}^2 \ge (n+2)^2.
\]
Adding \(1\) to both sides yields
\[
1+4t_{n+1}^2 \ge 1+(n+2)^2.
\]

The command `Real.sqrt_le_sqrt` is the monotonicity of the real square root: if \(a\le b\), then \(\sqrt a\le \sqrt b\). Applied here, since
\[
1+4t_{n+1}^2 \ge 1+(n+2)^2,
\]
it gives
\[
\sqrt{1+4t_{n+1}^2}\ge \sqrt{1+(n+2)^2}.
\]
Next, because
\[
1+(n+2)^2\ge (n+2)^2,
\]
the same monotonicity gives
\[
\sqrt{1+(n+2)^2}\ge \sqrt{(n+2)^2}.
\]
The proof has already shown \(0\le (n:\mathbb R)+2\), so `Real.sqrt_sq hn2_nonneg` rewrites \(\sqrt{(n+2)^2}\) as \(n+2\). Hence
\[
\sqrt{1+(n+2)^2}\ge n+2.
\]
The command `rw [Real.sqrt_sq hn2_nonneg] at h'` performs exactly this rewrite on the inequality produced by square-root monotonicity, and `exact h'` closes the goal with the desired orientation.

Finally, combining the unfolded formula
\[
t_{n+2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_{n+1}^2}}{2}
\]
with the two lower bounds
\[
\sqrt{1+4t_{n+1}^2}\ge \sqrt{1+(n+2)^2}\ge n+2
\]
yields
\[
t_{n+2}\ge \frac{1+(n+2)}{2}=\frac{n+3}{2}.
\]
The final `linarith` performs this linear combination. This proves the induction step \(P(n+2)\), and therefore `Nat.le_induction` concludes the theorem for the original \(k\) under the hypothesis \(1\le k\).
