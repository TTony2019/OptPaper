## Statement explanation
The declaration `theorem_4_1` is a convergence theorem for an alternating proximal-minimization scheme in a complete real Hilbert space \(H\). The two functions \(f,g:H\to\mathbb R\) are convex, lower semicontinuous, and bounded below. The fixed parameters satisfy \(\beta>0\) and \(\mu>0\), while the variable parameters \(\alpha_n,\nu_n\) are nonnegative. The two sequences \(x_n,y_n\) are generated by the two minimization inequalities \(h_{\mathrm{step},x}\) and \(h_{\mathrm{step},y}\), which say precisely that \(x_{n+1}\) minimizes
\[
\xi\mapsto f(\xi)+\frac{\alpha_n}{2}\|x_n-\xi\|^2+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\xi-y_n\|^2,
\]
and \(y_{n+1}\) minimizes
\[
\eta\mapsto g(\eta)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_{n+1}-\eta\|^2+\frac{\nu_n}{2}\|y_n-\eta\|^2.
\]
The hypothesis `h46` says that there exists at least one pair \((\bar x_0,\bar y_0)\) satisfying the `IsINE` equilibrium condition. Writing
\[
d(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\|\xi-\eta\|^2}{2},
\]
the condition
\[
\operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,\bar x,\bar y)
\]
means
\[
\forall \xi,\qquad
f(\bar x)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\bar x-\bar y\|^2
\le f(\xi)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\xi-\bar y\|^2,
\]
and
\[
\forall \eta,\qquad
g(\bar y)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bar x-\bar y\|^2
\le g(\eta)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bar x-\eta\|^2.
\]
Thus \(\bar x\) is a minimizer of \(f\) plus the quadratic coupling to \(\bar y\), and \(\bar y\) is a minimizer of \(g\) plus the quadratic coupling to \(\bar x\). The hypothesis `h47` is the summability of the positive variations of \(\alpha\) and \(\nu\):
\[
\sum_n \max(\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_n,0)<\infty,
\qquad
\sum_n \max(\nu_{n+1}-\nu_n,0)<\infty.
\]

The conclusion asserts the existence of a pair \((\bar x,\bar y)\in H\times H\) such that:
\[
x_n\rightharpoonup \bar x,
\qquad
y_n\rightharpoonup \bar y
\]
weakly in \(H\);
\[
\operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,\bar x,\bar y)
\]
holds for the limit pair;
the function
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(u,v)=\mu f(u)+\beta g(v)+\frac{\beta\mu}{2}\|u-v\|^2
\]
is globally minimized at \((\bar x,\bar y)\), i.e.
\[
\forall \xi,\eta,\qquad
L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y)\le L_{\beta,\mu}(\xi,\eta);
\]
the values converge,
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(x_n,y_n)\to L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y);
\]
the difference converges strongly,
\[
y_n-x_n\to \bar y-\bar x;
\]
and the individual objective values converge,
\[
f(x_n)\to f(\bar x),
\qquad
g(y_n)\to g(\bar y).
\]
So the theorem says that the alternating proximal scheme converges weakly to an equilibrium/minimizing pair, that the coupled energy \(L_{\beta,\mu}\) converges, that the difference of the two variables converges strongly, and that the two function values converge to their values at the limit.

    have h_nu_y : Tendsto (fun n : ℕ => ν n • (y_seq (n+1) - y_seq n))

## Proof explanation
The proof starts by storing the nonempty-equilibrium hypothesis `h46` and extracting a concrete pair \((x_0,y_0)\) together with the proof `hine₀` that this pair satisfies `IsINE`. It then abbreviates `x_seq` and `y_seq` by `x` and `y`. The positivity assumptions give \(\beta>0\), \(\mu>0\), and \(\beta\mu>0\). The first technical step rewrites the two algorithm inequalities into the forms `h_x_min` and `h_y_min`, where the quadratic coefficients are written as \(\alpha_n/2\), \(\beta/2\), \(\mu/2\), \(\nu_n/2\) and the norms are oriented in the way needed later. This is only a rearrangement: the tactic `intro` introduces the arbitrary \(n,\xi\) or \(n,\eta\), `rw` unfolds the abbreviations `x` and `y`, `simp [norm_sub_rev]` uses \(\|a-b\|=\|b-a\|\), and `nlinarith` closes the resulting arithmetic equivalence.

Next the proof extracts the two coordinate inequalities contained in `IsINE`. If a pair \((\bar x,\bar y)\) satisfies `IsINE`, then its first component gives
\[
f(\bar x)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\bar x-\bar y\|^2
\le f(\xi)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\xi-\bar y\|^2,
\]
and its second component gives
\[
g(\bar y)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bar x-\bar y\|^2
\le g(\eta)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|\bar x-\eta\|^2.
\]
These are recorded as `h_ine_x` and `h_ine_y`. The proof destructs the conjunction in `IsINE` with `rcases`, applies it to the chosen variable, and simplifies the terms involving \(\alpha=0\) or \(\nu=0\) and the self-distance \(\|\bar x-\bar x\|=0\).

The key estimate `ha` is the firm-nonexpansiveness inequality. For every `IsINE` pair \((\bar x,\bar y)\) and every \(n\), it proves
\[
\|x_{n+1}-\bar x\|^2
\le
\frac{1}{\alpha_n+\beta}
\Bigl(
\alpha_n\|x_n-\bar x\|^2
+\beta\|y_n-\bar y\|^2
-\alpha_n\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|^2
-\beta\|y_n-x_{n+1}-\bar y+\bar x\|^2
\Bigr).
\]
The proof sets \(x_{n+1}\), \(x_n\), \(y_n\), and \(\gamma=\alpha_n+\beta\), and notes \(\gamma>0\) from \(\alpha_n\ge0\) and \(\beta>0\). It applies `subgrad_of_min_quadratic` to the minimization defining \(x_{n+1}\). That lemma says: if a convex function \(f'\) is minimized at \(p\) for the quadratic expression
\[
\xi\mapsto f'(\xi)+\frac a2\|u-\xi\|^2+\frac b2\|\xi-v\|^2,
\]
then for every \(z\),
\[
f'(z)\ge f'(p)+\bigl\langle a(u-p)+b(v-p),\,z-p\bigr\rangle.
\]
Specialized here with \(p=x_{n+1}\), \(u=x_n\), \(v=y_n\), \(a=\alpha_n\), \(b=\beta\), it gives
\[
f(z)\ge f(x_{n+1})
+\bigl\langle \alpha_n(x_n-x_{n+1})+\beta(y_n-x_{n+1}),\,z-x_{n+1}\bigr\rangle.
\]
Similarly, applying `subgrad_of_min_quadratic` to the `IsINE` inequality for \(\bar x\) gives
\[
f(z)\ge f(\bar x)+\beta\langle \bar y-\bar x,\,z-\bar x\rangle.
\]
Combining these two subgradient inequalities at \(z=\bar x\) and \(z=x_{n+1}\) yields the inequality
\[
\alpha_n\langle x_n-x_{n+1},\bar x-x_{n+1}\rangle
+\beta\langle y_n-x_{n+1},\bar x-x_{n+1}\rangle
-\beta\langle \bar y-\bar x,\bar x-x_{n+1}\rangle\le 0.
\]
The proof then uses the Hilbert-space identity
\[
\langle u,v\rangle=\frac12\bigl(\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2-\|u-v\|^2\bigr)
\]
to expand the inner products, rearranges with `nlinarith`, and divides by \(\gamma>0\). The Lean steps `set`, `calc`, `rw`, `field_simp`, and `div_le_div_of_nonneg_right` implement these algebraic transformations.

The proof then packages the hypotheses into the structures required by Proposition 4.5. It builds an `H41toH46` record from convexity, lower semicontinuity, boundedness below, positivity of \(\beta,\mu\), and the nonempty `IsINE` hypothesis. It builds `h47_val : H47 α ν` directly from the two summability assumptions. It also defines an algorithm object `alg` with underlying sequences \(x_{\text{seq}}\) and \(y_{\text{seq}}\), whose step fields are exactly the two minimization inequalities. Then it invokes `proposition_4_5`. Specialized to the present data and to the known `IsINE` pair \((x_0,y_0)\), Proposition 4.5 gives:
\[
y_n-x_n\to y_0-x_0,
\]
\[
x_{n+1}-x_n\to0,
\qquad
y_{n+1}-y_n\to0,
\]
and the existence of real limits for \(\|x_{n+1}-x_0\|\) and \(\|y_{n+1}-y_0\|\). These consequences are stored as `h_yn_xn_conv`, `h_xdiff_conv`, `h_ydiff_conv`, `h_xnorm_conv`, and `h_ynorm_conv`.

From `H47`, the proof uses `bounded_of_H47_posPart`. That lemma says: if \(s_n\ge0\) for all \(n\) and the positive parts \(s_{n+1}-s_n\) are summable, then the sequence \(s\) is bounded above. Applying it to \(\alpha\) and \(\nu\) gives constants \(C_\alpha,C_\nu\) with \(\alpha_n\le C_\alpha\) and \(\nu_n\le C_\nu\). Since both sequences are nonnegative, this also gives \(|\alpha_n|\le C_\alpha\) and \(|\nu_n|\le C_\nu\). These bounds are used later to show that \(\alpha_n\) times a vanishing difference and \(\nu_n\) times a vanishing difference still tend to zero.

The proof then defines two candidate limit sets:
\[
S_x=\{\bar x\mid \exists \bar y,\ \operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,\bar x,\bar y)\},
\]
\[
S_y=\{\bar y\mid \exists \bar x,\ \operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,\bar x,\bar y)\}.
\]
Both are nonempty because of the initial `IsINE` pair. For every \(z\in S_x\), the proof applies Proposition 4.5 with the initial equilibrium pair \((z,y_z)\), where \(y_z\) witnesses \(z\in S_x\). This gives convergence of \(\|x_{n+1}-z\|\) to some real number \(l_z\); the lemma `tendsto_of_tendsto_nat_succ` then removes the shift and gives
\[
\|x_n-z\|\to l_z.
\]
Thus `hSx_dist` says that for every \(z\in S_x\), the distance from \(x_n\) to \(z\) converges. The symmetric argument gives `hSy_dist` for \(y_n\) and \(S_y\).

The longest technical part is the cluster-point argument `hSx_cluster`: every weak sequential cluster point of \(x_n\) lies in \(S_x\). Suppose \(z\) is such a cluster point. By the definition of `IsSequentialClusterPt`, there is a strictly increasing \(\varphi\) such that
\[
x_{\varphi(k)}\rightharpoonup z.
\]
The convergence \(\|y_{n+1}-y_0\|\to l_{y_0}\) implies, by `bounded_range_of_succ_distance_tendsto`, that the sequence \(y_n\) has bounded range. Hence the subsequence \(y_{\varphi(k)}\) is bounded, and `bounded_sequence_has_weakly_convergent_subsequence` gives a further strictly increasing \(\psi\) and a point \(\tilde y\) such that
\[
y_{\varphi(\psi(k))}\rightharpoonup \tilde y.
\]
Writing \(\chi=\varphi\circ\psi\), one then has both
\[
x_{\chi(k)}\rightharpoonup z,
\qquad
y_{\chi(k)}\rightharpoonup \tilde y.
\]
Using `weak_subseq_succ_of_step_tendsto_zero` together with \(x_{n+1}-x_n\to0\) and \(y_{n+1}-y_n\to0\), the shifted subsequences also satisfy
\[
x_{\chi(k)+1}\rightharpoonup z,
\qquad
y_{\chi(k)+1}\rightharpoonup \tilde y.
\]
The already known strong convergence \(y_n-x_n\to y_0-x_0\) gives
\[
y_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)}\to y_0-x_0.
\]
But weak convergence of the same difference gives
\[
y_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)}\rightharpoonup \tilde y-z.
\]
Strong convergence implies weak convergence, and uniqueness of limits in the weak topology gives
\[
y_0-x_0=\tilde y-z.
\]
The proof uses the injectivity of the map `toWeakSpace ℝ H` to pass from equality in the weak space back to equality in \(H\).

To show \(z\in S_x\), the proof must establish `IsINE` for the pair \((z,\tilde y)\). For the \(f\)-coordinate, it defines
\[
c_{x,k}
=
\alpha_{\chi(k)}\bigl(x_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)+1}\bigr)
+\beta\bigl(y_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)+1}\bigr).
\]
Applying `subgrad_of_min_quadratic` to the minimization of \(x_{\chi(k)+1}\) gives, for every \(w\),
\[
f(w)\ge f(x_{\chi(k)+1})
+\langle c_{x,k},\,w-x_{\chi(k)+1}\rangle.
\]
The first term of \(c_{x,k}\) tends to \(0\), because \(\alpha\) is bounded in absolute value and \(x_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)+1}\to0\); this uses `tendsto_smul_of_abs_bounded_of_tendsto_zero`. The second term satisfies
\[
y_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)+1}
=
\bigl(y_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)}\bigr)
+\bigl(x_{\chi(k)}-x_{\chi(k)+1}\bigr)
\to \tilde y-z.
\]
Therefore
\[
c_{x,k}\to \beta(\tilde y-z).
\]
Now `subdifferential_demiclosed_real_valued` is applied. This lemma says that for a convex lower semicontinuous \(f\), if \(x_k\rightharpoonup x\), \(u_k\to u\) strongly, and
\[
\forall k,z,\qquad f(z)\ge f(x_k)+\langle u_k,z-x_k\rangle,
\]
then
\[
\forall z,\qquad f(z)\ge f(x)+\langle u,z-x\rangle.
\]
Here it yields
\[
\forall w,\qquad f(w)\ge f(z)+\langle \beta(\tilde y-z),\,w-z\rangle.
\]
Then `subgrad_to_ine_f` converts this subgradient inequality into the quadratic inequality
\[
f(z)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|z-\tilde y\|^2
\le
f(\xi)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\xi-\tilde y\|^2
\]
for every \(\xi\). This is the first half of `IsINE` for \((z,\tilde y)\).

The \(g\)-coordinate is handled symmetrically. The proof defines
\[
c_{y,k}
=
\mu\bigl(x_{\chi(k)+1}-y_{\chi(k)+1}\bigr)
+\nu_{\chi(k)}\bigl(y_{\chi(k)}-y_{\chi(k)+1}\bigr).
\]
Applying `subgrad_of_min_quadratic` to the minimization of \(y_{\chi(k)+1}\) gives
\[
g(w)\ge g(y_{\chi(k)+1})
+\langle c_{y,k},\,w-y_{\chi(k)+1}\rangle
\]
for every \(w\). The first term tends to \(\mu(z-\tilde y)\), using the strong convergence of \(y_{\chi(k)+1}-x_{\chi(k)+1}\) and the equality \(y_0-x_0=\tilde y-z\). The second term tends to \(0\), using the boundedness of \(\nu\) and \(y_{\chi(k)}-y_{\chi(k)+1}\to0\). Hence
\[
c_{y,k}\to \mu(z-\tilde y).
\]
Another application of `subdifferential_demiclosed_real_valued`, now to \(g\), yields
\[
\forall w,\qquad g(w)\ge g(\tilde y)+\langle \mu(z-\tilde y),\,w-\tilde y\rangle.
\]
Then `subgrad_to_ine_g` converts this into
\[
g(\tilde y)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-\tilde y\|^2
\le
g(\eta)+\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-\eta\|^2
\]
for every \(\eta\). Together with the \(f\)-inequality, this proves
\[
\operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,z,\tilde y),
\]
so \(z\in S_x\). The proof `hSy_cluster` is the same argument with the roles of \(x\) and \(y\) interchanged.

At this point the hypotheses of `opial_lemma` are available for \(S_x\) and \(x_n\): the set \(S_x\) is nonempty, the distances \(\|x_n-z\|\) converge for every \(z\in S_x\), and every weak sequential cluster point of \(x_n\) belongs to \(S_x\). The Opial lemma states that under these conditions there exists \(z\in S_x\) such that \(x_n\rightharpoonup z\). The proof obtains
\[
x_n\rightharpoonup \bar x
\]
with \(\bar x\in S_x\). Applying the same lemma to \(S_y\) and \(y_n\) gives
\[
y_n\rightharpoonup \bar y
\]
with \(\bar y\in S_y\).

The next step is to show that the two witnesses can be taken to be the same equilibrium pair. Since \(\bar x\in S_x\), there is some \(y_x\) such that \((\bar x,y_x)\) satisfies `IsINE`. Applying Proposition 4.5 with this pair gives
\[
y_n-x_n\to y_x-\bar x.
\]
On the other hand, from the weak convergences \(x_n\rightharpoonup \bar x\) and \(y_n\rightharpoonup \bar y\), the difference converges weakly:
\[
y_n-x_n\rightharpoonup \bar y-\bar x.
\]
Strong convergence implies weak convergence, so uniqueness of limits gives \(y_x-\bar x=\bar y-\bar x\), hence \(y_x=\bar y\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{IsINE}(f,g,d,d,d,0,\beta,\mu,0,\bar x,\bar y)
\]
holds. Applying Proposition 4.5 once more to this pair gives the final strong convergence
\[
y_n-x_n\to \bar y-\bar x.
\]

The global minimality of \(L_{\beta,\mu}\) at \((\bar x,\bar y)\) is obtained from `ine_minimizes_L_beta_mu`. This lemma says that if \(f,g\) are convex, \(\beta,\mu>0\), and \((\bar x,\bar y)\) satisfies the same `IsINE` condition with the squared-distance coupling, then
\[
\forall \xi,\eta,\qquad
L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y)\le L_{\beta,\mu}(\xi,\eta).
\]
So the equilibrium pair is a global minimizer of the coupled objective.

The convergence of the \(L\)-values is proved using the perturbation inequality `L_beta_mu_perturbation_ineq`. That lemma states that under the convexity and positivity hypotheses, if \(x_{n+1}\) and \(y_{n+1}\) are the two minimizers described by `h_x_min` and `h_y_min`, then for every \(n,\xi,\eta\),
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})
\le
L_{\beta,\mu}(\xi,\eta)
+\Bigl\langle \mu\bigl(\alpha_n(x_{n+1}-x_n)+\beta(y_{n+1}-y_n)\bigr),\,\xi-x_{n+1}\Bigr\rangle
+\Bigl\langle \beta\nu_n(y_{n+1}-y_n),\,\eta-y_{n+1}\Bigr\rangle.
\]
Taking \(\xi=\bar x\) and \(\eta=\bar y\), the two inner-product correction terms are
\[
\Bigl\langle \mu\bigl(\alpha_n(x_{n+1}-x_n)+\beta(y_{n+1}-y_n)\bigr),\,\bar x-x_{n+1}\Bigr\rangle
\]
and
\[
\Bigl\langle \beta\nu_n(y_{n+1}-y_n),\,\bar y-y_{n+1}\Bigr\rangle.
\]
The proof shows both correction vectors tend to \(0\). Indeed, \(x_{n+1}-x_n\to0\), \(y_{n+1}-y_n\to0\), and \(\alpha_n,\nu_n\) are bounded in absolute value, so the products \(\alpha_n(x_{n+1}-x_n)\) and \(\nu_n(y_{n+1}-y_n)\) tend to \(0\). The factors \(\bar x-x_{n+1}\) and \(\bar y-y_{n+1}\) tend weakly to \(0\), hence have bounded ranges by `bounded_range_of_tendsto_weakly`. The lemma `tendsto_inner_zero_of_tendsto_zero_of_bounded` then says that the inner product of a strongly vanishing sequence with a bounded sequence tends to \(0\). Thus the two correction inner products tend to \(0\).

Meanwhile, `h_minimizing` gives the lower bound
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y)
\le
L_{\beta,\mu}(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})
\]
for every \(n\). The perturbation inequality and the vanishing correction terms give the eventual upper bound: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), eventually
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})
\le
L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y)+\varepsilon.
\]
The lemma `tendsto_of_lower_bound_and_eventual_upper` then yields
\[
L_{\beta,\mu}(x_{n+1},y_{n+1})\to L_{\beta,\mu}(\bar x,\bar y).
\]
Finally `tendsto_of_tendsto_nat_succ` removes the shift and gives the stated convergence of \(L_{\beta,\mu}(x_n,y_n)\).

From this \(L\)-convergence, the proof subtracts the already-known convergence of the purely quadratic term
\[
\frac{\beta\mu}{2}\|x_n-y_n\|^2.
\]
Indeed, \(y_n-x_n\to \bar y-\bar x\) gives \(x_n-y_n\to \bar x-\bar y\), and continuity of the norm squared gives
\[
\frac{\beta\mu}{2}\|x_n-y_n\|^2
\to
\frac{\beta\mu}{2}\|\bar x-\bar y\|^2.
\]
Subtracting this from the \(L\)-convergence yields the weighted convergence
\[
\mu f(x_n)+\beta g(y_n)
\to
\mu f(\bar x)+\beta g(\bar y).
\]

To obtain the individual limits of \(f(x_n)\) and \(g(y_n)\), the proof uses weak lower semicontinuity. The lemma `weakly_seq_lsc_of_convex_lsc` says that if \(f\) is convex and lower semicontinuous and \(x_n\rightharpoonup \bar x\), then for every \(\varepsilon>0\), eventually
\[
f(x_n)\ge f(\bar x)-\varepsilon.
\]
The same holds for \(g\) and \(y_n\). Then `first_value_tendsto_of_weighted_sum_and_lsc` is applied. That lemma says: if \(\mu,\beta>0\), if
\[
\mu a_n+\beta b_n\to \mu a_0+\beta b_0,
\]
and if eventually \(a_n\ge a_0-\varepsilon\) and \(b_n\ge b_0-\varepsilon\) for every \(\varepsilon>0\), then
\[
a_n\to a_0.
\]
Taking \(a_n=f(x_n)\), \(b_n=g(y_n)\), \(a_0=f(\bar x)\), \(b_0=g(\bar y)\) gives
\[
f(x_n)\to f(\bar x).
\]
Swapping the roles of the two weights and the two sequences gives
\[
g(y_n)\to g(\bar y).
\]

Finally the proof assembles all obtained facts into the existential conclusion: the weak limits \(\bar x,\bar y\), the weak convergence of \(x_n\) and \(y_n\), the `IsINE` condition for \((\bar x,\bar y)\), the global minimality of \(L_{\beta,\mu}\) at this pair, the convergence of \(L_{\beta,\mu}(x_n,y_n)\), the strong convergence of \(y_n-x_n\), and the convergence of \(f(x_n)\) and \(g(y_n)\).
