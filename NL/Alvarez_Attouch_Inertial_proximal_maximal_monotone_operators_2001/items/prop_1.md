## Lean statement
```lean
theorem inertial_proximal_point_converges_of_monotone_parameters
    {A : SetValuedOperator H H} (hA : A.IsMaximalMonotone)
    (x : ℕ → H) (alpha lambda : ℕ → ℝ)
    (hiteration : InertialProximalPointAlgorithm A x alpha lambda)
    (hzeros : A.zeros.Nonempty)
    (hlambda : ∃ lambda0 : ℝ, 0 < lambda0 ∧ ∀ k : ℕ, lambda0 ≤ lambda k)
    (halpha : ∃ alpha0 : ℝ, 0 ≤ alpha0 ∧ alpha0 < 1 / 3 ∧
      (∀ k : ℕ, 0 ≤ alpha k ∧ alpha k ≤ alpha0) ∧ Monotone alpha) :
    Summable (fun k : ℕ => ‖x (k + 1) - x k‖ ^ 2) ∧
      ∃ xhat : H, xhat ∈ A.zeros ∧
        Tendsto (fun k => toWeakSpace ℝ H (x k)) atTop (𝓝 (toWeakSpace ℝ H xhat))
```

## Statement explanation
The declaration concerns an ambient space $H$ and a set-valued operator $A$ on $H$, represented as `SetValuedOperator H H`. Thus $A$ assigns to each point $u\in H$ a subset $A(u)\subseteq H$. The hypothesis `hA : A.IsMaximalMonotone` says that $A$ is maximal monotone: its graph is monotone and cannot be properly enlarged while remaining monotone. In the usual Hilbert-space setting, this is the structural assumption that makes the proximal/resolvent steps well behaved.

The variable $x:\mathbb N\to H$ is the sequence of iterates, and I write $x_k$ for $x(k)$. The functions $\alpha:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$ and $\lambda:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$ are, respectively, the inertial parameters and the proximal step sizes. The hypothesis `hiteration : InertialProximalPointAlgorithm A x alpha lambda` says that $x$ is generated by the inertial proximal point algorithm for $A$ with these parameters. Its exact recurrence is not shown in the provided source, but the proof uses it only through its update component: it records the proximal/inertial update relation satisfied by the sequence.

The hypothesis `hzeros : A.zeros.Nonempty` says that the zero set of $A$ is nonempty. Concretely, there exists $z\in H$ such that $0\in A(z)$, i.e. $z$ is a zero of the maximal monotone operator. The hypothesis `hlambda` says that there is a positive constant $\lambda_0>0$ such that

$$
\lambda_0 \le \lambda_k
$$

for every $k\in\mathbb N$. Thus the step sizes are uniformly bounded below by a positive number. The hypothesis `halpha` says that there is $\alpha_0$ with

$$
0\le \alpha_0<\frac13,
$$

such that for every $k\in\mathbb N$,

$$
0\le \alpha_k\le \alpha_0,
$$

and in addition $\alpha$ is monotone, meaning that for all $i,j\in\mathbb N$,

$$
i\le j \implies \alpha_i\le \alpha_j.
$$

So the inertial coefficients are nonnegative, nondecreasing, and uniformly bounded above by a constant strictly less than $1/3$.

The conclusion is a conjunction. First, the real series

$$
\sum_{k\in\mathbb N} \left\|x_{k+1}-x_k\right\|^2
$$

is summable; equivalently, the total squared successive increments of the iterates is finite. Second, there exists $\hat x\in H$ such that

$$
\hat x\in A.\mathrm{zeros},
$$

i.e. $0\in A(\hat x)$, and the sequence $x_k$ converges weakly to $\hat x$. The weak convergence is expressed by the Lean conclusion

$$
\mathrm{Tendsto}\!\left(k\mapsto \mathrm{toWeakSpace}_{\mathbb R,H}(x_k)\right)\ \mathrm{atTop}\ \left(\mathcal N\!\left(\mathrm{toWeakSpace}_{\mathbb R,H}(\hat x)\right)\right),
$$

which means that the images of $x_k$ in the weak space converge to the image of $\hat x$ as $k\to\infty$. In ordinary terms: under maximal monotonicity of $A$, existence of a zero, uniformly positive lower bounded step sizes, and nonnegative nondecreasing inertial parameters bounded by a constant strictly below $1/3$, the inertial proximal point algorithm has finite total squared movement and converges weakly to some zero of $A$.

## Lean proof
```lean
by
  have halg : InertialProximalAlgorithm A x alpha lambda := by
    intro k hk
    exact hiteration.update k hk
  exact sufficient_condition_for_convergence hA x alpha lambda halg hzeros hlambda halpha
```

## Proof explanation
The proof has two parts.

First, it establishes an auxiliary hypothesis

$$
\texttt{halg} : \texttt{InertialProximalAlgorithm}\ A\ x\ \alpha\ \lambda.
$$

This is a weaker or repackaged form of the original algorithm assumption: it asks only for the update relation needed by the convergence criterion, not for the full structure of `InertialProximalPointAlgorithm`. The proof of `halg` begins with `intro k hk`. This introduces an arbitrary index $k$ and the side condition $hk$ under which the auxiliary algorithm predicate requires an update statement at $k$. The goal after `intro k hk` is therefore the update relation at that index.

The next line, `exact hiteration.update k hk`, uses the original hypothesis `hiteration`. The hypothesis says that $x$ is generated by the full inertial proximal point algorithm. Its component `update`, applied to $k$ and $hk$, gives exactly the update relation demanded at index $k$. Thus `halg` is obtained by extracting the concrete update information from `hiteration` and forgetting any additional packaging present in the stronger algorithm predicate. No algebraic rewriting, simplification, or linear arithmetic is used here; the proof is purely a structural extraction of the needed hypothesis.

Second, the proof applies the cited result `sufficient_condition_for_convergence` to the arguments

$$
hA,\quad x,\quad \alpha,\quad \lambda,\quad \texttt{halg},\quad
\texttt{hzeros},\quad \texttt{hlambda},\quad \texttt{halpha}.
$$

The visible role of this theorem is as a sufficient-condition lemma. It takes as input the maximal monotonicity of $A$, the sequence $x$, the two parameter sequences, the auxiliary inertial proximal algorithm relation, the existence of a zero of $A$, the uniform positive lower bound on the step sizes $\lambda_k$, and the bounded monotone condition on the inertial parameters $\alpha_k$. From these hypotheses it returns exactly the target conclusion: summability of

$$
\left\|x_{k+1}-x_k\right\|^2
$$

and the existence of a zero $\hat x\in A.\mathrm{zeros}$ such that $x_k$ converges weakly to $\hat x$. Since the complete statement of `sufficient_condition_for_convergence` is not included in the provided source, the explanation uses precisely the consequence visible at the call site: it converts the collected hypotheses into the theorem’s final conjunction.

Finally, `exact sufficient_condition_for_convergence ...` closes the original goal because the term produced by that theorem has exactly the required type. In this proof the Lean commands only package and apply hypotheses: `have` introduces the auxiliary fact `halg`, `intro` proves the universal auxiliary condition pointwise, and `exact` terminates each goal by supplying a term of the exact required type. There is no use of `rw`, `simp`, or `linarith` in this proof.
