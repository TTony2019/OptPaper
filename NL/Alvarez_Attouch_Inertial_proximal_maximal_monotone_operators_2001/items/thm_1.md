## Lean statement
```lean
theorem inertial_proximal_point_converges
    {A : SetValuedOperator H H} (hA : A.IsMaximalMonotone)
    (x : ℕ → H) (alpha lambda : ℕ → ℝ)
    (hiteration : InertialProximalPointAlgorithm A x alpha lambda)
    (hzeros : A.zeros.Nonempty)
    (hlambda : ∃ lambda0 : ℝ, 0 < lambda0 ∧ ∀ k : ℕ, lambda0 ≤ lambda k)
    (halpha : ∃ alpha0 : ℝ, 0 ≤ alpha0 ∧ alpha0 < 1 ∧
      ∀ k : ℕ, 0 ≤ alpha k ∧ alpha k ≤ alpha0)
    (hsummable : Summable (fun k : ℕ => alpha (k + 1) * ‖x (k + 1) - x k‖ ^ 2)) :
    ∃ xhat : H, xhat ∈ A.zeros ∧
      Tendsto (fun k => toWeakSpace ℝ H (x k)) atTop (𝓝 (toWeakSpace ℝ H xhat))
```

## Statement explanation
The declaration `inertial_proximal_point_converges` states a weak convergence theorem for the inertial proximal point algorithm associated with a set-valued operator $A$ on an ambient space $H$. The operator $A$ is a set-valued operator from $H$ to $H$, and the hypothesis `hA : A.IsMaximalMonotone` says that $A$ is maximal monotone. The sequence $x : \mathbb N \to H$ is the sequence of iterates. The two sequences $\alpha, \lambda : \mathbb N \to \mathbb R$ are real parameter sequences: $\lambda$ plays the role of the proximal step-size sequence, and $\alpha$ plays the role of the inertial/momentum parameter sequence. The hypothesis `hiteration : InertialProximalPointAlgorithm A x alpha lambda` says that $x$ is generated by the inertial proximal point algorithm for $A$ with these parameters. The exact update formula is not reproduced in the visible source, but the proof later extracts from this hypothesis the pointwise update information at each relevant index.

The hypothesis `hzeros : A.zeros.Nonempty` says that the zero set of $A$ is nonempty: there exists at least one $z \in H$ such that $0 \in A z$. The hypothesis `hlambda` says that there is a real number $\lambda_0 > 0$ such that $\lambda_k \ge \lambda_0$ for every $k \in \mathbb N$. Thus the proximal step sizes are uniformly bounded below by a positive constant. The hypothesis `halpha` says that there is an $\alpha_0$ with $0 \le \alpha_0 < 1$ such that, for every $k$,

$$
0 \le \alpha_k \le \alpha_0 < 1.
$$

Thus the inertial parameters are nonnegative and uniformly bounded above by a number strictly less than $1$. The hypothesis `hsummable` says that the series

$$
\sum_{k\in\mathbb N} \alpha_{k+1}\,\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|^2
$$

is summable. This is a finite discrete energy condition on the successive differences of the iterates, weighted by the next inertial parameter.

The conclusion is that there exists $\hat{x} \in H$ such that $\hat{x} \in A.zeros$, i.e.

$$
0 \in A\hat{x},
$$

and such that the sequence $x_k$, regarded as an element of the weak space over $\mathbb R$ via `toWeakSpace`, converges to $\hat{x}$ in the weak topology. In ordinary language: under maximal monotonicity of $A$, existence of a zero of $A$, a positive uniform lower bound on the proximal step sizes, inertial parameters uniformly bounded in $0 \le \alpha_k \le \alpha_0 < 1$, and summability of the weighted squared increments, the inertial proximal point iterates converge weakly to a zero of $A$.
