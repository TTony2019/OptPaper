## Statement explanation
The theorem `rem_5_1` is a conditional convergence-rate statement for a sequence generated by an inertial proximal algorithm. The ambient space is a real Hilbert space $H$, with inner product $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ and norm $`\|\cdot\|`$.

The hypotheses are the following.

- $A$ is a set-valued operator from $H$ to $H$. The assumption $hA$ says that $A$ is maximal monotone. The assumption $hdomA$ says that $\mathrm{dom}(A)=H$, so $A$ is defined on the whole space. The assumption $hAzero_int$ says that

$$
0\in\mathrm{interior}(A(0)).
$$

  Thus the value of $A$ at the origin is a set containing an open ball around $0$.

- $L:H\to H$ is a continuous linear map. The hypothesis $hL_symm$ says that $L$ is self-adjoint:

$$
\langle Lx,y\rangle=\langle x,Ly\rangle
\quad\text{for all }x,y\in H.
$$

  There is a real number $m\gt 0$, and $hL_coercive$ says that

```math
\langle Lx,x\rangle\ge m\|x\|^2
\quad\text{for all }x\in H.
```

  Hence $L$ is a positive definite self-adjoint continuous linear operator with coercivity constant $m$.

- Let $\sigma_{A(0)}$ be the support function of the set $A(0)$:

$$
\sigma_{A(0)}(y)=\sup_{a\in A(0)}\langle a,y\rangle.
$$

  The hypothesis $h_condition$ says that the function

```math
x\mapsto \mathrm{excess}\!\left(A(x),\,\partial\sigma_{A(0)}(x)+\{Lx\}\right)
```

  is $`O(\|x\|^2)`$as$x\to 0$. In words, near the origin the set$A(x)$is approximated by the set$\partial\sigma_{A(0)}(x)$, shifted by the singleton$`\{Lx\}`$, with an error of quadratic order in$`\|x\|`$.

- $f:H\to\mathbb R$ is a function. The assumptions say that $f$ is convex on all of $H$, is $C^1$, and is bounded below: there is a real lower bound $c$ with $f(x)\ge c$ for all $x\in H$. There is also a real parameter $M$ such that $f$ is strongly convex with parameter $M$.

- $B$ is a set-valued operator. The hypothesis $hB_eq_grad$ says that $B$ has domain $H$ and is the singleton-valued gradient operator of $f$:

```math
B(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\}
\quad\text{for every }x\in H.
```

- There is a real number $\lambda\gt 0$. The sequence $\lambda_n:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R$ is constant with value $\lambda$, so $\lambda_n=\lambda$ for every $n$. The sequence $x:\mathbb N\to H$ is a trajectory of the algorithm named `AlgorithmA` with operators $A$ and $B$ and step sizes $\lambda_n$. Finally, $x_{\mathrm{Inf}}$ is a limit of the sequence:

$$
x_n\to x_{\mathrm{Inf}}
\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.
$$

The conclusion is that there exist $n_0\in\mathbb N$ and real numbers $K,q$ such that

$$
K>0,\qquad q>0,\qquad q<1,
$$

and for every $n\ge n_0$,

```math
\|x_{n+1}-x_n\|\le K q^n.
```

Since $0\lt q\lt 1$, the right-hand side decays geometrically. Thus the theorem says that, under the stated monotonicity, coercivity, strong-convexity, and quadratic-approximation hypotheses, the consecutive differences of the algorithm eventually decay at a geometric rate. This is an eventual linear convergence statement for the increments of the sequence.
