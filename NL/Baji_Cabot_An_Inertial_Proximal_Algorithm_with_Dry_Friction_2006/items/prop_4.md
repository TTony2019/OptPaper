## Statement explanation
The declaration `prop_5_1` is a theorem about a real Hilbert space \(H\), two set-valued operators \(A,B:H\rightrightarrows H\), a convex differentiable function \(f:H\to\mathbb R\), a positive nondecreasing sequence \((\lambda_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\), and a sequence \((x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) generated by the algorithm associated with \(A,B,\lambda\). The operator \(A\) is assumed maximal monotone, has full domain \(H\), satisfies \(0\in \operatorname{int}(A(0))\), and has \(A(0)\) a convex polyhedron. There is also an asymptotic compatibility condition near \(0\):
\[
\operatorname{excess}\!\left(A(x),\ \partial \sigma_{A(0)}(x)\right)\longrightarrow 0
\quad\text{as }x\to 0.
\]
Here \(\sigma_C\) is the support function of \(C\),
\[
\sigma_C(z)=\sup_{y\in C}\langle y,z\rangle,
\]
understood in extended real values, and \(\partial \sigma_C(x)\) is the subdifferential of \(\sigma_C\) at \(x\). The set-theoretic excess used here is
\[
\operatorname{excess}(P,Q)=\sup_{p\in P}\operatorname{dist}(p,Q).
\]
Thus the hypothesis says that, as \(x\) approaches \(0\), every point of \(A(x)\) is asymptotically close to the subdifferential of the support function of \(A(0)\) at \(x\).

The function \(f\) is assumed convex on all of \(H\), continuously differentiable, and bounded below. The operator \(B\) is exactly the gradient operator of \(f\), so that \(B(y)=\{\nabla f(y)\}\) for every \(y\in H\). The sequence \((\lambda_n)\) is strictly positive and nondecreasing. The sequence \((x_n)\) satisfies the algorithm relation `AlgorithmA A B x lam` and converges to a point \(x_\infty\).

The conclusion is that eventually the discrete velocity \(x_{n+1}-x_n\) lies in the normal cone to the value set \(A(0)\) at the point \(-\nabla f(x_\infty)\). More precisely,
\[
\exists\, n_*,\ \forall n\ge n_*,\qquad
x_{n+1}-x_n\in N_{A(0)}\bigl(-\nabla f(x_\infty)\bigr).
\]
The normal cone is encoded as
\[
N_C(\bar x)=\left\{u\in H:\ \bar x\in C
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sup_{y\in C}\langle u,y-\bar x\rangle\le 0\right\}.
\]
Since the proof first shows that \(-\nabla f(x_\infty)\in A(0)\), the conclusion says that the limiting point is a stationary point in \(A(0)\), and the eventual increments of the algorithm are normal directions to the polyhedron \(A(0)\) at that point.

## Proof explanation
The proof begins by setting
\[
C:=A(0),\qquad \bar x:=-\nabla f(x_\infty).
\]
It first proves \(\bar x\in C\). This is exactly the content of `prop51_stationary_gradient_mem_A_zero`: under maximal monotonicity of \(A\), full domain, \(0\in \operatorname{int}(A(0))\), convexity and \(C^1\)-regularity and boundedness below of \(f\), the assumption that \(B\) is the gradient operator of \(f\), positivity and nondecrease of \((\lambda_n)\), the algorithm relation, and convergence \(x_n\to x_\infty\), one has
\[
-\nabla f(x_\infty)\in A(0).
\]
Thus, after unfolding \(C\) and \(\bar x\), the proof obtains \(\bar x\in C\).

Next the proof defines two derived sequences:
\[
v_n:=(\lambda_n)^{-1}\bigl(x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\bigr),
\]
\[
a_n? \qquad
\operatorname{acc}_n:=(\lambda_n^2)^{-1}\bigl(x_{n+2}-2x_{n+1}+x_n\bigr).
\]
The theorem `prop51_scaled_velocity_tendsto_zero` says that for positive nondecreasing \((\lambda_n)\) and \(x_n\to x_\infty\),
\[
(\lambda_n)^{-1}\bigl(x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\bigr)\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Hence \(v_n\to 0\). Similarly, `prop51_acceleration_tendsto_zero` says
\[
(\lambda_n^2)^{-1}\bigl(x_{n+2}-2x_{n+1}+x_n\bigr)\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Hence \(\operatorname{acc}_n\to 0\).

The proof next uses `prop51_algorithm_step_selection`. This lemma extracts from the algorithm relation `AlgorithmA A B x lam` the existence, for every \(n\), of points \(a_n,b_n\in H\) such that
\[
a_n\in A(v_n),\qquad b_n\in B(x_{n+2}),
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{acc}_n+a_n+b_n=0.
\]
The Lean command `choose` replaces the repeated existential statement by actual sequences \(a_n,b_n\) satisfying these properties.

The proof then identifies \(b_n\). Since \(B\) is the gradient operator of \(f\), `prop51_gradient_operator_mem_eq` gives, from \(b_n\in B(x_{n+2})\),
\[
b_n=\nabla f(x_{n+2}).
\]
Then `prop51_gradient_shift_tendsto`, using \(C^1\)-regularity of \(f\) and \(x_n\to x_\infty\), gives
\[
\nabla f(x_{n+2})\longrightarrow \nabla f(x_\infty).
\]
Therefore \(b_n\to \nabla f(x_\infty)\).

From the equation \(\operatorname{acc}_n+a_n+b_n=0\), the proof rewrites
\[
a_n=-\operatorname{acc}_n-b_n.
\]
Since \(\operatorname{acc}_n\to 0\) and \(b_n\to \nabla f(x_\infty)\), continuity of negation and subtraction gives
\[
a_n\longrightarrow -\nabla f(x_\infty)=\bar x.
\]
The algebraic rearrangement is handled by `abel`, with `simpa` rewriting definitions.

The proof then defines
\[
S_n:=\partial \sigma_C(v_n),
\]
the subdifferential of the support function of \(C=A(0)\) at \(v_n\). It needs to know that each \(S_n\) is nonempty. The supplied proof contains a `sorry` at exactly this point. Before the `sorry`, the proof derives a bound as follows. From \(a_n\in A(v_n)\) and \(y\in C=A(0)\), maximal monotonicity of \(A\) gives
\[
0\le \langle a_n-y,\ v_n-0\rangle.
\]
Rewriting \(v_n-0=v_n\) and expanding the inner product gives
\[
0\le \langle a_n,v_n\rangle-\langle y,v_n\rangle,
\]
hence
\[
\langle y,v_n\rangle\le \langle a_n,v_n\rangle
\]
for every \(y\in C\). Thus the support function \(\sigma_C(v_n)\) is bounded above by the finite real number \(\langle a_n,v_n\rangle\). The `linarith` tactic performs this rearrangement. However, the conclusion that \(S_n=\partial\sigma_C(v_n)\) is nonempty is left as `sorry`; the source comment identifies the missing ingredient as finite-halfspace support attainment for the polyhedron \(C\). Therefore the proof as displayed is not complete: everything after this point depends on that unsolved subgoal. No proof of that missing statement is supplied.

Assuming that \(S_n\) is nonempty, the proof obtains from the global hypothesis `h_condition` and from \(v_n\to 0\) that
\[
\operatorname{excess}\bigl(A(v_n),S_n\bigr)\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Indeed `h_condition` says
\[
\operatorname{excess}\bigl(A(x),\partial\sigma_{A(0)}(x)\bigr)\to 0
\]
as \(x\to 0\), and \(v_n\to 0\), so this is composition with the convergent sequence.

Next, `prop51_normalCone_stability_convexPolyhedron` is applied to the convex polyhedron \(C=A(0)\) at the point \(\bar x\in C\). It gives a neighborhood \(V\) of \(\bar x\) such that for every \(z\in V\),
\[
N_C(z)\subseteq N_C(\bar x).
\]
Using `Metric.mem_nhds_iff`, the proof shrinks \(V\) to a metric ball \(B(\bar x,\varepsilon)\) contained in \(V\), with \(\varepsilon>0\).

The proof then uses `prop51_full_domain_local_bounded`. Since \(A\) is maximal monotone and has full domain, it is locally bounded at \(0\): there exists \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
A\bigl(B(0,\delta)\bigr)
\]
is bounded. The proof then observes the following eventual facts:
- \(v_n\in B(0,\delta)\) eventually, because \(v_n\to 0\);
- \(a_n\in B(\bar x,\varepsilon/8)\) eventually, because \(a_n\to \bar x\);
- \(\operatorname{excess}(A(v_n),S_n)\le \varepsilon/8\) eventually, because the excess tends to \(0\).

For such an index \(n\), the proof applies `prop51_bddAbove_infDist_of_local_bounded`. This lemma says that if \(A\) is bounded on \(B(0,\delta)\), if \(v\in B(0,\delta)\), and if a set \(S\) is nonempty, then the set of distances
\[
\left\{\operatorname{dist}(y,S): y\in A(v)\right\}
\]
is bounded above. Here \(v=v_n\) and \(S=S_n\). This uses the nonemptiness of \(S_n\) supplied by the earlier `sorry`.

Then `prop51_excess_point_le_of_bdd` is applied with \(a_n\in A(v_n)\), with the boundedness of the range of distances from \(A(v_n)\) to \(S_n\), and with
\[
\operatorname{excess}(A(v_n),S_n)\le \varepsilon/8.
\]
It concludes
\[
\operatorname{dist}(a_n,S_n)\le \varepsilon/8.
\]
Next, `prop51_exists_mem_dist_le_infDist_add` says that if \(S_n\neq\varnothing\), if \(\eta>0\), and if \(\operatorname{infDist}(a_n,S_n)\le r\), then there exists \(\xi\in S_n\) with
\[
\operatorname{dist}(a_n,\xi)\le r+\eta.
\]
Taking \(r=\varepsilon/8\) and \(\eta=\varepsilon/8\), the proof obtains \(\xi\in S_n\) such that
\[
\operatorname{dist}(a_n,\xi)\le \varepsilon/4.
\]

The proof then shows \(\xi\in V\). By the triangle inequality,
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\xi,\bar x)\le \operatorname{dist}(\xi,a_n)+\operatorname{dist}(a_n,\bar x).
\]
We have \(\operatorname{dist}(\xi,a_n)\le \varepsilon/4\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(a_n,\bar x)<\varepsilon/8\), hence
\[
\operatorname{dist}(\xi,\bar x)<\varepsilon.
\]
Thus \(\xi\in B(\bar x,\varepsilon)\subseteq V\). This inequality is handled by `nlinarith`.

Since \(\xi\in S_n=\partial\sigma_C(v_n)\), `prop51_subderivative_supportFunction_mem_normalCone` gives
\[
v_n\in N_C(\xi).
\]
Because \(\xi\in V\), the stability inclusion \(N_C(\xi)\subseteq N_C(\bar x)\) yields
\[
v_n\in N_C(\bar x).
\]
The proof then uses `prop51_normalCone_smul_nonneg`: if \(u\in N_C(\bar x)\) and \(a\ge 0\), then \(a u\in N_C(\bar x)\). Since \(\lambda_n>0\), this gives
\[
\lambda_n v_n\in N_C(\bar x).
\]
Finally, from \(v_n=(\lambda_n)^{-1}(x_{n+2}-x_{n+1})\), the scalar cancellation gives
\[
\lambda_n v_n=x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}.
\]
The cancellation is performed by `field_simp`, using \(\lambda_n\neq 0\) from \(\lambda_n>0\). Hence, eventually,
\[
x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\in N_C(\bar x).
\]

The proof converts this eventual statement into an index \(N\): for all \(n\ge N\),
\[
x_{n+2}-x_{n+1}\in N_C(\bar x).
\]
It then chooses \(n_*=N+1\). To prove the desired statement for every \(n\ge N+1\), it considers two cases. If \(n=0\), then \(0\ge N+1\) is impossible, and `omega` closes the goal. If \(n=m+1\), then \(m+1\ge N+1\) implies \(m\ge N\) by `Nat.succ_le_succ_iff.mp`. Applying the eventual statement at \(m\) gives
\[
x_{m+2}-x_{m+1}\in N_C(\bar x).
\]
Since \(n=m+1\), this is exactly
\[
x_{n+1}-x_n\in N_C(\bar x).
\]
Unfolding \(C=A(0)\) and \(\bar x=-\nabla f(x_\infty)\) gives the claimed conclusion
\[
x_{n+1}-x_n\in N_{A(0)}\bigl(-\nabla f(x_\infty)\bigr).
\]

Thus, apart from the unsolved `sorry` asserting nonemptiness of the subdifferentials \(S_n=\partial\sigma_C(v_n)\), the proof shows that eventually the discrete velocities of the algorithm are normal to the polyhedron \(A(0)\) at the stationary limit point \(-\nabla f(x_\infty)\).
